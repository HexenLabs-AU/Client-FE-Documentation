Open **Contracts** to switch between **Offers**, **Federal**, and **Relationships**. Corporation contracts have their own [contract board](/docs/gameplay/corporation-contracts).

## Review business offers and relationships

Business offers and negotiation work through posting freight. In **Offers**, compare the commodity, quantity, pickup, destination, distance, estimated loads, delivery rates, completion bonus, and expiry before accepting work.

Use the available **Accept**, **Deny**, and counteroffer controls. **Agreed Deals** shows accepted work. The **Relationships** tab shows business relationships and trust labels such as **Preferred Carrier**.

Use each live offer for its terms rather than expecting a fixed route, payout, or offer schedule.

## Set contract dispatch priority

**Set Dispatch Priority to Contracts for contract work to run through automatic dispatch.** Check the company default and any individual driver override in **Dispatch → Auto Dispatch**.

An Advanced Dispatcher can order corporation contracts, business deals, and Federal Contracts. See [Dispatcher priorities](/docs/gameplay/dispatchers#choose-priorities-and-contract-order).

## Offline contract progress

<Warning>
  **No contracts progress offline.** Business deals, Federal Contracts, and corporation contracts all require online progress.
</Warning>

## Roll and host a Federal Contract

<Steps>
  <Step title="Roll offers">
    Open **Contracts → Federal → Contracts** and select **Roll Contracts**. The panel shows **Next Free Roll**, **Odds**, and **How It Works**; a **Reroll** control is also available. Check its availability and any displayed cost.

    <Frame>
      <img
        src="/images/guides/federal-roll-menu.png"
        alt="Federal Contracts menu with Roll Contracts, Reroll, Odds, How It Works, My Contract, Board, and History"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the offer">
    Read the required company level, freight, routes, workload, security deposit, potential payout, time limit, and special requirements. Compare the workload with your fleet capacity. These values depend on the offer.

    <Frame>
      <img
        src="/images/guides/federal-offer-details.png"
        alt="Example Federal offer with company-level requirement, payout, security deposit, workload, destinations, time limit, and special requirements"
      />
    </Frame>

    _Example offer shown. Its level requirement, payout, deposit, workload, and five-hour time limit describe that offer; check the one you roll._
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose who can join">
    Configure **Minimum company level** and **Who can join**. The shown choices are **Public** or **Invite Only**.

    <Frame>
      <img
        src="/images/guides/federal-post-settings.png"
        alt="Federal offer posting controls for minimum level and public or invite-only joining, with Post to the Board"
      />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post and recruit">
    Select **Post to the Board**. Partners can join while the listing is recruiting. **Posting does not start the delivery timer or take the security deposit.**
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start when ready">
    Open **My Contract**, review the participants, then select **Start Contract**. **Starting takes the security deposit and begins the displayed time limit.** The timer is not extended.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Manage a recruiting listing

**My Contract** shows participants, hauled freight, contribution percentages, and estimated payouts. **Host Controls** manages the listing. **Cancel Listing** removes a recruiting listing from the board; the shown screen states that joined participants lose nothing when that listing is cancelled.

The listing has its own expiry countdown, separate from the delivery timer that begins when you start the contract.

## Join another company's Federal Contract

1. Open **Contracts → Federal → Board**.
2. Compare the host, route, freight, equipment, deposit, minimum level, participant spaces, and capacity requirements.
3. Select **Join** on an eligible recruiting listing and review the current terms.

## Understand payment and failure

<Note>
  **Federal Contracts pay only at the end, not after each load.** When the contract is successfully completed, payout is split by the freight each company hauled. Hauling nothing earns nothing.
</Note>

**The security deposit is returned on successful completion.** Keep money available for running costs until the contract pays.

<Warning>
  **A failed Federal Contract pays nothing. There is no partial-completion payout, and the deposit is lost.** Complete the contract before its deadline.
</Warning>
